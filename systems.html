How the simulation works

Simple to watch. Layered underneath — and every layer pushes on the others.

WorldForge is not a set of independent features. It is one chain of causes: the land decides what can live, what lives decides what can be built, and what is built decides who holds power — while war, hunger and time push back down the chain.

The systems

Working today

Terrain & geography

Every world is generated from a seed: continents, mountain ranges, forests, plains and frozen wastes. Landform decides fertility and movement, so civilizations thrive where the land allows — and the terrain model was adopted only after a 100-seed comparison showed it no worse than what it replaced, on every measure.

Working today

Ecology

Grazers eat, predators hunt, and both populations rise and fall by the actual food web — no scripted booms or busts. Humans live inside the same web: villages forage the land around them, and a stripped landscape starves real settlements.

Working today

Settlements

People found villages, raise houses (each house is a real structure on the map), and stockpile food and wood. A village's territory follows its built footprint. Houses can burn in raids; a village with empty stores for too long is abandoned, and its people migrate or scatter.

Working today

Kingdoms & rulers

Villages band into kingdoms under named rulers whose lifespans and succession are part of the world's record. Each kingdom has a stability score built from things a player can check — food stores, peace, calm borders, reign continuity — and low stability has consequences: when a ruler dies at the wrong moment, a kingdom can lose villages to rivals or watch its best-fed village break away as a new crown.

Working today

Diplomacy

Kingdoms that touch borders keep a relationship: grievances from wars and raids, truces with reasons, alliances, and allies called to war. Every major event lands in the chronicle with the motive that produced it, so a relationship's history can be read rather than guessed.

Working today

Warfare

Wars are fought by mustered levies fed from the home granary — an army that outruns its food stops being an army. Wars have declared goals (conquest of land, or punishment), truces cost tribute, and sustained occupation annexes villages outright. Distance and supply are real constraints, measured in the test panels.

Designed — implementation paused

Living diplomacy

Grievances that grow without any war — from contested borders and competition for the same fields — and alliances that form around shared threats in ordinary play. The design is complete and evidence-based; building it waits until the observatory (below) is finished, so its behaviour can be properly measured.

Planned

Trade, settlements & seasons

Inter-kingdom trade and caravans; richer settlements with more building types, roads and walls; seasons and climate acting on farming and survival; rivers with mechanical effects. Each is sequenced behind the systems it must interact with — trade needs money and professions, roads grow out of trade routes.

In development — current work

The observatory

Not a gameplay system: the instrument panel for all of them. A truth panel that shows why every value holds, a permanent history record for every world, autopsy tools for collapses, and long-horizon health monitoring across many worlds. It is read-only by construction — it can never change the world it observes. See the roadmap for its five phases.

Design rules

  • Determinism: one seed grows one world and one history, reproducibly. That is what makes every claim on this site testable.
  • Interaction over isolation: a new system must push on the existing ones, or it does not ship.
  • No scripted history: events happen because the rules produced them, never because a timeline says so.
  • Proportionate models: mathematics earns its place by producing believable behaviour, not by looking sophisticated.

System statuses on this page follow the development record: “working today” means implemented and verified in a development build; “planned” means designed or sequenced, not built. Updated at milestone closeouts.
