Evidence & quality

How this project knows what it claims to know — and what it admits it doesn't.

Three grades of “it works”

Every claim about the simulation carries one of three grades. The grades are not interchangeable, and this site uses them literally.

1 · Implemented

The code exists and the automated suite passes — currently 1,060 checks across the ordinary and safety-instrumented builds, plus fuzz testing of the save format. Implementation proves a mechanism can work. It says nothing about whether the living world actually uses it.

2 · Demonstrated

A controlled, reproducible experiment shows the behaviour happening for the stated reason. Example: warfare was accepted after twelve worlds × 30,000 days produced 896 war onsets and 80 conquests through the designed mechanisms — counted by instruments, not inferred from anecdotes.

3 · Established

The behaviour holds across varied conditions and the wider simulation is unharmed. Example: the current terrain model was adopted only after a 100-seed matched comparison showed it non-inferior on every endpoint — and the two seeds where it did worse are documented by name, not averaged away.

Claims are allowed to fail

A relationship described in a design document but not found by measurement stays described — it is never promoted by enthusiasm. Standing example: the warfare design expected distance from home to change how occupation plays out. Measured at every supply setting the design allowed, it did not. That is recorded as an open limitation in the project's register, with a design rethink owed — not tuned until the numbers confessed.

Known gaps — shown, not hidden

Open case · #33

The world that died unexplained

A randomly seeded world went extinct in every species by about year 39 while running unattended. Its fate reproduces exactly from its seed — but its 19 history entries were never written to disk, so why it died is unknown. The case is registered as future test material for the observatory's autopsy tools. Unknown means unknown until the evidence exists.

Technical debt · TD-5

History is kept in a rolling window

The chronicle retains the most recent 1,024 entries — enough for recent events, not for centuries. The permanent history record (observatory Phase B) is the fix, designed around completeness, consistency with what actually happened, and fast retrieval. Until it ships, long-horizon history is honestly partial.

Invariant gap · INV-2

One purity proof is indirect

Recording history must never disturb the simulation's randomness. That is proven directly for the relief system; for history-writing itself, the current proof is indirect (identical chronicles across save/load). The observatory's first phase must either close this with a direct test or report it as still open.

Measured limitation

Kingdoms cap at 24

Kingdoms only disappear when their last village does, so long-lived worlds eventually fill all 24 kingdom slots and founding stops — while wars, successions and defections continue. Measured, classified as a design decision owed in the long-term milestone, and not counted as a defect until its gameplay cost is judged. The simulation does not halt at the cap: one test world recorded 82,602 births over 30,000 days in that state.

Long-horizon evidence

Ecology over centuries

Some long test worlds collapse ecologically over 30,000 days; others saturate. Whether that is defensible turnover or a defect is an open judgment, assigned to the long-term milestone with the evidence preserved. It is not being quietly tuned in the meantime.

Where the records live

The game's source repository is private during development, so this site carries the public record: milestone closeouts, this page's gap register, and the roadmap are all drawn from the same development log the team works from — never written separately for show. When the game ships, the repository and its full evidence archive ship with it.

If a claim on this site ever conflicts with the development record, the record is right and the site gets corrected.
